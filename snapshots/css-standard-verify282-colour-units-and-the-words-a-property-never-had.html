b282 — a colour argument is not always a number. 'lch(50% 30 120deg)' carries a percentage in the lightness, an angle in the hue and a number in the chroma, and the engine prints all three in their own way: the lightness as the number the percentage means ('lab(50% …)' is 'lab(50 …)' while 'oklab(50% …)' is 'oklab(0.5 …)', because the two spaces measure lightness on different scales), the angle in degrees without the unit ('50grad' is '45'), and an alpha as a fraction ('/ 50%' is '/ 0.5'). A 'none' channel is printed as 'none' and computes as the zero it stands for. The colour space 'xyz' is an alias and the engine says the long name, 'xyz-d65'. This was not only a spelling: the parser met '120deg' where it wanted a number, read nothing, and painted that lch() BLACK. Beside it, a channel written as a percentage used to be rounded into a byte by truncation, so 'hsl(120 50% 50% / 25%)' echoed 0.247 and 'hwb(120 20% 30%)' lost the half of its green; both round to nearest now, a tie away from zero, which is what the reference engine reports. A 'color-mix()' whose second component carries the percentage says the FIRST one instead — 'red, blue 40%' is 'red 60%, blue' — because the first has no initial to hide behind. Then four properties that were never quite there: 'speak' (which the reference engine declares and accepts 'none', 'normal' and the four manuals of, and whose initial is 'normal'), the shape trio 'shape-outside'/'shape-margin'/'shape-image-threshold' (a shape with its box printed behind it, a length that resolves against the element's own font, a number a percentage spells as its fraction — and a bare box on its own IS a shape-outside value, the rectangle of that box), and the predefined counter styles, which are keywords and print in their one spelling while a name that is not one of them keeps the author's case ('DISC' is 'disc', 'HEX' is 'HEX'). 'grid-area' was the layout bug of the round: one value is the row-start alone — 'span 2' never spanned anything in the other three lines — and only a line NAME is the named-area form that fills all four; 'grid-column: span 2' also stopped printing the ', auto' it does not mean.
